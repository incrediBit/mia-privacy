Privacy Policy for MIA

App: MIA (pk.mia.dialer) · Last updated: 3 November 2026

In short: MIA is an offline photo dialer. It does not collect, transmit, sell, or share your personal data. The names, phone numbers, and photos you add stay in the app's private storage on your own phone. MIA has no internet permission, no account, no ads, and no analytics.

Why MIA exists

MIA was built for people who cannot read or write — including people who cannot read numbers. For them an ordinary phone is out of reach: contact lists, keypads, menus, and settings all assume reading. MIA replaces all of that with faces. A helper adds each person once (name, number, and a photo), and from then on calling is a single tap on a familiar face — no reading, no typing, no menus. That single purpose is also why MIA keeps everything on the device, works offline, and asks for as little as possible.

1. Who we are

MIA is an Android application developed by Kamran Taheri ("we", "us", "our"). This policy explains how the app handles information. It applies to the MIA app distributed through Google Play and to this policy page.

2. Information you add to the app

To use MIA, a helper enters a name and a phone number and chooses a photo for each person. Those three things are saved only in MIA's private storage on your device:

  • Name and phone number you type or paste, saved in a small local file.
  • Photo you pick from the gallery. MIA copies and compresses a resized copy into its private folder. Your original photo is not modified or moved.

This information is never sent to us or to anyone else. MIA does not ask for or read your device's contact list, call history, messages, location, camera, or microphone. MIA does not have the Android INTERNET permission, so the app itself is technically unable to transmit data off your device.

3. Device permissions and why they are used

  • CALL_PHONE — used only when you tap a contact's CALL button, to place the call directly instead of opening the dialer.
  • READ_MEDIA_IMAGES (Android 13+) / READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE (Android 12 and older) — used only so you can choose a photo for a contact.

These permissions are used only for the features described above. You can deny or revoke them in Android Settings; photo picking and calling will then be unavailable, but the rest of the app keeps working.

4. Collection, sharing, and selling

We collect nothing and we share nothing. We do not operate a server that receives app data. MIA contains no advertising, analytics, crash-reporting, or other third-party SDKs, and we do not sell or rent any data. Because no data is collected, there is no data to disclose, transfer, or sell to third parties.

5. Device backups

MIA allows Android's built-in backup feature (allowBackup). If you have backup enabled on your device, Android may include MIA's local data (names, numbers, and contact photos) in the backup of your phone that is stored in your own Google account. That backup is controlled by you and Google under Google's Privacy Policy; we cannot access it. You can turn device backup off in Android Settings, and deleting MIA's data or uninstalling the app removes it from the device (existing cloud backups expire according to Google's backup retention rules).

6. Children

MIA is a general-purpose communication utility intended for adults. It is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. In fact, the app collects no personal information from anyone.

7. Keeping and deleting your data

You are in control of the data on your device:

  • Delete one contact: press and hold the small delete button on the contact's card for about 2 seconds. The contact's local entry and copied photo are deleted.
  • Delete everything: uninstall MIA, or go to Settings → Apps → MIA → Storage → Clear data. This removes all names, numbers, and photos that MIA stored.

We keep no copy, so there is nothing for us to delete on our side and no deletion request to send us.

8. Security

MIA's data sits in the app's private storage area, which Android protects from other apps. Because MIA does not transmit data, there is no data in transit to intercept. As with any app, someone with physical access to an unlocked phone, or a rooted or compromised device, may be able to read local app data.

9. Visiting this policy page

This page is a static web page. It sets no cookies and runs no analytics. Our hosting provider may automatically log standard technical request data (such as IP address, browser type, and time of access) to operate and secure the service, as is normal for web servers.

10. Changes to this policy

If MIA's data practices change, we will update this page and change the "Last updated" date above before the change takes effect. If a future version of MIA ever collects data, the policy and the Google Play Data safety section will be updated to describe it.
